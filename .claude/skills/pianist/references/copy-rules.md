# Copy Rules: Headlines, Buttons, First Screen

## Research: five leading pianist sites against weaker portfolios

**Benchmark set (top five in the niche):** Yuja Wang, Igor Levit, Daniil Trifonov, Khatia Buniatishvili, Víkingur Ólafsson. These are internationally active soloists whose official sites are run by labels and management.
**Comparison set (weaker):** typical self-built portfolios of emerging pianists on Wix/Squarespace templates, and teacher-performer hybrid sites.

> **Method note:** the network policy in the build environment blocked live fetches of these sites. The observations below come from search results and the long-standing structure of these sites, not a fresh crawl. Artist sites get redesigned every season or two, so re-check before quoting any specific wording. The *patterns* are stable across the genre and are what the rules encode.

### What the strong sites do on the first screen

| # | Pattern | Seen across the top five | What weaker portfolios do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The name is the headline.** The artist's name, set large, is the dominant type. No tagline sits above or beside it. | All five | "Welcome to my website", or a slogan ("Music is my life") that outranks the name |
| 2 | **One photograph, one person.** A single, art-directed portrait or performance image, often low-key or monochrome. | All five | Carousels of 4–6 mixed photos, stock images of piano keys, busy collages |
| 3 | **Lead with the newest concrete thing.** The latest album (title + label + "out now") or the next concert (date, city, hall). | Ólafsson, Trifonov and Wang lead with the latest release. Levit and Buniatishvili lead with recordings and dates. | A paragraph about loving music since age five. No dates, no titles |
| 4 | **Buttons name an action on an object.** "Listen", "Watch", "Tickets", "All dates", "Discography". One or two at most. | All five | "Learn more", "Click here", "Read more", "Submit", "Discover", or five equal buttons |
| 5 | **Nav is single nouns.** Schedule/Concerts, Recordings/Discography, Media/Video, Biography, Contact. Five to seven items. | All five | Long or cute labels ("My Musical Journey", "Let's Connect!"), dropdowns nested two deep |
| 6 | **Dates are promoted.** The schedule is on the first or second screen, formatted like a programme: date · city · venue · repertoire · ticket link. | All five | Concerts buried on a sub-page, or listed as plain text with no venue or link |
| 7 | **Praise is borrowed, not claimed.** Short press quotes with a named outlet. Self-description sticks to facts (label, orchestras, halls). | Most, especially in bios and on release pages | Self-applied adjectives: "world-class", "breathtaking", "passionate", "virtuoso" |
| 8 | **Bio in third person, facts first.** Opens with what the artist does now, not childhood. Full bio sits one click deeper. Press kit downloadable. | All five | First-person life story above the fold, with no download for presenters |
| 9 | **Restraint in chrome.** Monochrome or near-monochrome UI. The photo and the type carry the mood. | All five | Gradients, multiple accent colours, decorative music-note icons, script fonts |
| 10 | **Audience segmentation is quiet.** Fans get Listen/Watch. Presenters get management contact and a press kit. Neither shouts. | All five | One generic contact form for everyone, sometimes as the hero |

### Weaker-portfolio habits to avoid (seen repeatedly)
- A headline that describes the site ("Official website of…") instead of the artist.
- Stacked job titles as the hero: "Pianist | Composer | Educator | Collaborator".
- Autoplaying audio or video with sound.
- Every section ending in "Learn more".
- Paragraph-heavy first screens. The visitor has to read 80+ words before learning anything concrete.
- Generic AI-sounding phrasing: "embark on a musical journey", "a tapestry of sound", "elevate your event".

## The rules (apply to everything `/pianist` writes)

### A. Structure
1. **One idea per screen.** A screen is a full-height section with one headline, at most one supporting sentence, and at most one primary action plus one secondary action. If you need "and", you need another screen.
2. **First screen = name + one current fact + one action.** For example: SRINIJAKARA / "Next: Rachmaninoff's Second, Wigmore Hall, 14 November." / **Tickets**. Nothing else above the fold except the nav.
3. **Order the homepage by what a visitor came to do:** Hear her → See where she's playing → Know what she plays → Know who she is → Get in touch.
4. **The schedule is never more than one screen from the top.**

### B. Headlines
5. **2–6 words.** Specific nouns over abstractions: "Rachmaninoff, live" beats "Musical Excellence".
6. **No slogans as H1.** The page H1 is the name. Section headlines say what is on the screen: "Upcoming concerts" is fine, and "Hear her live" is better. "Where Passion Meets Performance" is not acceptable.
7. **Concrete over claimed.** Write what happened or what will happen (a work, a hall, a date, a label), never how good it was.
8. **No question headlines, no exclamation marks, no colons in headlines.**

### C. Supporting lines (lead)
9. **One sentence, 20 words or fewer.** It adds exactly one fact the headline doesn't carry.
10. **Show with specifics:** composers, works, venues, cities, collaborators, years.
11. **Body paragraphs are 45 words or fewer** (Bebas Neue body needs short blocks). Longer content lives one click deeper.

### D. Buttons & links
12. **Verb + object, 1–3 words:** Tickets · Listen · Watch the performance · All concerts · Read the biography · Press kit (PDF) · Book a concert · Join the list.
13. **Banned button labels:** Learn more · Click here · Read more · Discover · Explore · Submit · Get started · Check it out · Find out more.
14. **One primary button per screen.** A secondary action is a text link with →.
15. **Say where a link goes** when it leaves the site: "Listen on Spotify →", "Tickets at Wigmore Hall →".

### E. Voice
16. **Third person in the bio** ("Srinijakara performs…"). First person only in a short, signed note from the artist.
17. **Calm, assured, specific.** Write like a concert programme read aloud by someone who was there. Present tense for what she does, past tense for what she did.
18. **Borrowed praise only:** press quotes must be real and attributed: `— Outlet, Year`. Never invent quotes, reviews, venues, awards, collaborators or dates. Use `[placeholder]` brackets when facts are unknown.
19. **Numbers as numerals** in UI ("14 Nov", "3 recordings"). Spell out in flowing prose if under ten.
20. **British/International spelling is fine** (programme, colour) if used consistently. Choose one per project.

### F. Banned vocabulary (AI-sounding or empty)
Never use these words or phrases (or close variants):

> passionate · passion · journey · musical journey · embark · elevate · unleash · unlock · delve · dive into · immerse · immersive · tapestry · symphony of (used figuratively) · harmony of (used figuratively) · resonate · resonates deeply · evoke · captivating · captivate · mesmerizing · mesmerising · breathtaking · soulful · enchanting · magical · stunning · vibrant · dynamic · world-class · renowned (unless quoting press) · virtuoso (unless quoting press) · seamless · curated · timeless · transcend · transcendent · testament to · realm · landscape (figurative) · beacon · navigate · boasts · showcase (as a verb in copy) · nestled · "in today's world" · "more than just" · "not just X, but Y" · "where X meets Y" · "whether you're X or Y" · "look no further" · "it's no secret" · "a true" · "truly" · "deeply" · "incredibly" · "a celebration of" · "brings to life" · "takes you on"

Also avoid: stacked triplets of adjectives, rhetorical questions, exclamation marks, em-dash chains, "Welcome to…", and job-title stacks with pipes.

> Note: the project brief itself uses "passionate pianist" and "musical journey". Those describe the *brief*, not the copy. On the site, show the commitment through specifics (hours, works, halls) instead.

### G. Self-check before shipping copy
- [ ] Does every screen pass the "one idea" test? Can you state it in five words?
- [ ] Is the H1 the name?
- [ ] Does the first screen carry a concrete, dated fact and one action?
- [ ] Zero banned words (search the output for each)?
- [ ] Every button is verb + object, and none is banned?
- [ ] Every claim is a fact or an attributed quote. Unknowns are `[placeholders]`.
- [ ] Every paragraph is 45 words or fewer, and every lead line 20 words or fewer?
